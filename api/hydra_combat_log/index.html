Expand description

Combat recorded, passively: nothing is ever sent to the game.

The combat reader in the model assembles each chunk’s attacks (their rolls, flares, hits and crits) and the facts beside them (statuses, spell losses, stuns, deaths). This crate writes them to the ledger as hunts: a hunt opens at the first combat event and closes after IDLE_TIMEOUT seconds without one, stamped at the last event’s time, so time in town never pads it. ours on an attack or flare is the reader’s word; kill_credit says how a creature’s killer was chosen.

  • recorder: the facts into rows, on a connection of its own.
  • task: the recorder on a session, and its idle clock.
  • report: the hunts and their numbers, as lines.
  • detail: the trees: attacks and creatures one by one.
  • listing: every attack name or creature over a scope, a page at a time.
  • analytics: flares, defences, HP, control, aim, efficiency, compared.
  • command: .cstats, parsed and answered.
  • catalog: the reports named, for a window that lists them.

Modules§

analytics
The analytics: a flare’s behaviour, a creature’s defences, HP estimates, control and blind coverage, aim and accuracy, kill efficiency, and two hunts compared. These use only recorded facts: a missing status event is not a failure, and a requested aim location is an assumption, never history.
catalog
The reports named, for a window that lists them: each with the words that ask for it, a line saying what it shows, and what it takes.
command
.cstats on the command line: what was asked, and its answer from the ledger.
detail
The feeds and the trees: recent attacks and creatures, one creature’s attack tree, one attack’s detail, one spawn tree.
listing
The listings: every attack name or creature over a scope, a page at a time, by damage or newest first.
recorder
The facts of one chunk into the ledger’s rows.
report
What the hunts show, as lines: queries over the ledger’s combat tables.
task
The recorder on a session: every chunk’s combat facts the session broadcasts go to a thread of the recorder’s own, which writes them; a tick every half minute lets it close a hunt gone idle.
