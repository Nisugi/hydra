Who said what, and on which channel.

The wire is the only authority here, and it turns out to say more than enough.

§The markup already carries the channel and the speaker

<preset id='speech'><a exist="-10007833" noun="Pukk">Pukk</a> says</preset>, "Have fun!"
<preset id="whisper"><a exist="-10807620" noun="Calvix">Calvix</a>
whispers,</preset> "See you there"

Three facts, all structural:

FactWhere
the channelthe <preset id=>
the speakerthe exist link inside it
the bodythe run after the preset closes

So this classifier re-tokenizes nothing, which is the reason it is twenty lines rather than a pile of regexes. A text-matching version would have to decide what says means in prose, and the corpus shows why that fails: <X> whispers a quiet invocation and <X> whispers arcane incantations are spell-casting emotes, not speech. They carry no preset, so nothing here mistakes them for messages.

MEASURED over 208 recorded sessions: 203 speech and 3 whisper presets, and those are the only two preset ids in the corpus.

The wire uses both quoting styles for the preset’s id – the whisper lines use double quotes – which text::attribute already handles and a hand-rolled scan for single quotes would not.

§Verbs are kept, not enumerated

The verb phrase is whatever sits between the speaker and the close: says, asks, exclaims, and also softly, squeakily, monotonously, darkly. MEASURED: 14 distinct openers over 206 lines, and the tail is plainly adverbial rather than a closed set. So Message::verb is the text, and the channel — the part that is closed — is the preset.
