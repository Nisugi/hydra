The six combat stances, and the percent bands they occupy.

The names, the bands and reading a name. The sending half is not here — changing stance, and waiting for the game to accept or decline, needs a connection and roundtime, so it belongs above this crate.

The reader is needed before any sender: the band table answers “am I in the stance I asked for?”, which a behavior must do before it sends anything.

value= and the percent inside text= agreed in all 19,526, so Stance::from_percent over either gives the same answer. Only three of the six names appear; the other three are the game’s too, and a character who never stood in them cannot evidence them.

§READING ACCEPTS ANY PERCENT; SETTING IS RESTRICTED TO TENS

A stance you may ASK FOR is a multiple of ten. That is a rule about setting, and putting it in the reader would reject a real game state.

The corpus proves it: defensive (99%) occurs, and both readings come from the same moment — a gold-bristled hinterboar feints to the left. You spot the ruse too late as you move to block — so a game event can knock the stance off a round number. A reader that accepted only multiples of ten would have called that unknown, or worse, snapped it to 100.

So Stance::from_percent takes any 0..=100, and the multiple-of-ten rule belongs with the sender, where it is a caller-argument check.
