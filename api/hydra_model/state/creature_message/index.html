Module creature_message 

Source
Expand description

Matching a game line against the bestiary’s messages: every kind – arrival, flee, death, decay, the attacks, the triggers, the casting preparations, standing up, shaking off a stun, searching, the description and the ambient flavor.

§The gap this closes

creature_messages.tsv holds 7,158 lines, joined onto every creature by Creature::messages_of. This is what matches a line against them.

A match of an MessageKind::Attack or MessageKind::Trigger line also says which: the attack’s name, or the effect – bind, web, silence – the creature is about to land. That is the cause behind a symptom, available before the symptom.

§What this is for, and what it is NOT for

It is not how arrivals, departures and hides are told apart. Leaving a room carries a direction in <d>; arriving does not, and hiding does not. MEASURED over eight combat logs: of the lines carrying a creature link, all 195 departures carry a direction <d>, and 0 of 142 arrivals and 0 of 38 hides do. So departure.rs reads all three apart from the markup alone.

It is not the earliest death signal either. <crtrStatus> carries dead="1" at the start of the blob, and the death line is usually towards the end – the status tag and the prose are in one blob, the tag first. <crtrStatus dead="1"> is the authority on death and this is not. MEASURED for what it is worth: 184 dead= attributes across three logs, every one inside a room objs component.

So what this is for is narrow: it recognises a creature’s prose as prose – which a log reader, a transcript or a highlight rule wants, and which nothing else can do. It is not how a behavior learns that something died.

§Who calls it

The story marks a creature’s own lines with it – its death, a spell it is preparing, a special about to land, its recovery – for the theme to colour (hydra-ui’s creature_line). Nothing in this crate calls it: wiring death prose into Creatures would mean choosing between it and <crtrStatus> as the authority on one creature’s death, a decision with no caller to justify it.

§Placeholders, and why matching is not a plain compare

MEASURED: 2,633 of the 7,158 lines carry a placeholder – 1,119 attack, 802 flee, 171 death, 162 arrival, 121 spell prep, 88 stun break, the rest scattered – so two thirds of flee lines cannot be compared as text:

An Agresh bear lumbers {direction}.
An Agresh bear slowly backs away, {pronoun} teeth bared.

Each placeholder has a closed list of forms, and a form missing from a list makes an otherwise-correct message unmatchable – its and their (possessives) and out (a flee direction) must be there.

The template’s literal text is matched as is, and each {name} as one of its forms. It is done without a regex engine – the template is split on its placeholders and the parts are matched in order, which is the same decision afflictions.rs made for the same reason.

§{target} and {weapon} are wildcards

MEASURED: 362 lines carry {target} and 337 carry {weapon}, most of them attacks. Neither has a closed list of forms.

Both are treated here as wildcards over a single segment: any run of text with no line break. The alternative is silently never matching 362 real lines.
