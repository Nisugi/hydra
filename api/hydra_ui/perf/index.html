Module perf 

Source
Expand description

How quick a character’s window is, stage by stage: a line from the bytes arriving to its display, and a command from Enter to the game’s response displayed, shown per character on the hub.

Each stage keeps its last KEPT samples, and says their median and their 95th percentile: one slow line is a hiccup, a slow median is the problem.

  • Line: a chunk of the game’s bytes arriving, to the first frame that painted a line of it. While Lich’s text is shown, the line is Lich’s copy of the game’s, matched to it by its text. Split in three:
    • Feed: the bytes arriving, to the line in the story;
    • Wait: in the story, to the window’s next frame starting;
    • and that frame, to its play window drawn.
  • Frame: one whole frame of the windows: the hub, every play window, every other window open.
  • Lich: the game’s bytes arriving, to Lich’s copy of the line coming back; Lich’s text shown only.
  • Enter -> sent: a typed line, to its being written to the game: through Lich, when one runs.
  • Sent -> reply: written, to the next of the game’s bytes arriving: the game and the network, which Hydra cannot make quicker.
  • Reply -> screen: those bytes arriving, to the frame that painted the next line shown.
  • Paint: how long the play window took to draw one frame.

Times are this process’s monotonic clock, which every stage shares; a frame painted is the end of the window’s drawing of it, so a sample is early by at most what the display then takes to show it.

STAGES
Each stage, in the table’s order. Its marks are what a person feels, and what Hydra should do: a line on the screen within 30 ms of its bytes reads as at once, and Hydra alone takes 10-20; past 100 ms it is a lag. A frame is due every 16 ms at 60 a second. The game’s answer is lag as players mean it, the network’s and the game’s, coloured all the same.
