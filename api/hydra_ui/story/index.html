Expand description

One character’s story as its window shows it: the game’s lines, its prompts and what was sent; each stream’s own lines, for a widget of that stream; and what Hydra says of the connection, for Hydra’s own pane.

Hydra’s own news is never among the game’s lines: its pane is an info window into what Hydra is doing, and its errors.

§Lines are assembled here, from the session’s frames

The parser gives one text frame per markup boundary and marks the last of each wire line with ends_line; the frames are gathered per stream until one ends the line, as the model gathers them (hydra-model’s lines.rs).

§Where a stream’s line goes

Every line not on the main stream is kept on its own stream (Streams), for a widget of it: that widget is the stream’s window, open. The story takes it too when the game sends it to the main window while that window is closed, as the game declared with <streamWindow ifClosed= styleIfClosed=> (Windows::route): into the story (logons, death), into the story in a style (thoughts, familiar), or nowhere (room, inv, speech: panels, or copies of what main already says). A story line of another stream is kept as Shown::From, so a play window with that stream’s widget open leaves it out of the story. The declarations are read off the frames as they come, so a line is routed by what was declared before it.

§Highlights

Each line the game finishes, the story’s and every stream’s, is looked at once by the character’s highlights (Story::highlight_with) before anything else takes it: painted, hidden, or calling for attention. A banner is kept here for the window to show (Story::alerts_at); a sound or a notification waits for whoever plays them (Story::calls). Each highlight calls at most once in its cooldown.

§A creature’s own lines

A line that is a creature’s death, its spell coming, a special about to land or its recovery, as the bestiary records them, takes that kind’s preset before the highlights see it (crate::creature_line), so the theme colours it as it colours speech.

§The combat reader’s word

When the game’s prompt closes a chunk, the session says which of its lines the combat reader found to be a flare, a status landing on the character or a killing blow ([Event::Marked]); the story finds each among the main lines it showed in that chunk, by its text, and gives it the preset (crate::combat_line). The reader matched them once; the story matches nothing again. In Lich’s view the marks come before Lich’s copy of the chunk does, and each waits for its line.

§The player’s Lich

While the player’s Lich runs, the story shows one of two texts ([View]), switched between as a toggle:

  • Lich’s ([View::Lich], the default): what Lich shows a frontend, so its squelches hold and its scripts’ words show. The game’s own lines are still looked at, for the highlights’ sounds, banners and notifications alone, so those answer the game whatever Lich hid.
  • The game’s ([View::Game]): the game’s own text, and Lich’s own lines beside it. A Lich that marks what it forwards (--sentinel) says which are its own; one that does not is guessed at: a line the game itself said lately is the game’s, and is not shown twice.

A Lich started late shows again the login it was handed; that is left out, up to its prompt. Lich echoes its scripts’ commands itself ([go2]>north), so the story does not.

§Prompts

The game sends a prompt with every chunk, one of effects alone among them. A prompt is shown after the story has had a line with something to read, or when it changed (R> to >), never twice in a row for nothing. A blank line the game sent is its formatting and is shown; a line left blank by a highlight hiding its words is not. The live prompt drops its R once roundtime has run out (Story::settle): the game sends no prompt when it ends.
