The player’s own Lich, through the relay: what it may do to the character it runs for, through LichDoor, which hydra-lich holds.

Hydra keeps the game’s connection. Lich, started in pipe mode and pointed at a port the relay holds, takes the relay as its game. Once attached it is handed the game’s bytes as they came, and what the player types that Hydra does not take; what it writes to that port comes here as lines to send (LichDoor::send).

§The bytes, not the lines

Lich parses the stream itself, markup and all, so it is handed exactly what the game sent, as the recorder is: copied where a chunk arrives, before the parser, and read by nothing here.

§The player’s typing

The symbol a line starts with says whose it is: a line starting with Lich’s command symbol goes to Lich, one starting with Hydra’s goes to Hydra. The rest of what the player types goes to Lich too, for its aliases and hooks.

So a line is Hydra’s first, by its symbol (claimant). Then:

  • a line with Lich’s symbol (SessionHandle::lich_symbol, ; unless the player chose another) is Lich’s, handed to its standard input, and never Hydra’s or the game’s: with no Lich running, the player is told;
  • what the player typed at a window (SessionHandle::send_typed) is Lich’s while one runs, and Lich sends what it makes of it;
  • Hydra’s own lines (a relayed .to’s) go to the game, past Lich’s hooks, as a Lich script’s put never meets them.

§Kept up, and let go

The Lich attached is the session’s, not a connection’s, so it stays up through a reconnect and sees the new login as more of the stream. Its scripts cannot work with no game connection, so while there is none its lines are not sent, and the relay says so.

A Lich that stops reading is let go rather than waited on, or fed a stream with a hole in it: the copy holds WIRE_CHUNKS chunks, and the first that finds it full ends it (Wire::fell_behind). Dropping chunks would leave a hole Lich cannot recover from, and blocking would stall the game’s own reader, so neither is done.

§What it shows

What Lich would show a frontend, its standard output, is handed back (LichDoor::show), parsed by a parser of its own (text.rs), and published as Event::LichShown, each frame marked with whose line it was. A window shows it in place of the game’s text while Lich runs (View::Lich: Lich’s squelches hold and its scripts’ messages show), or the game’s own text and Lich’s own lines beside it (View::Game); the player toggles between the two. The session’s own parse of the game is everything else, as it was: the model, the panels, the log.

§Started late

A Lich attached after the login is handed a login first, built from what the session knows as it attaches (GameState::login), then the live stream. The session’s actor builds it, from its model, between one chunk and the next, so the Lich misses nothing and is told nothing twice.

§Claimed while it starts

Ruby and Lich take from seconds to half a minute to start. The relay claims the character’s Lich as it starts one: what the player types for it waits for it from then on, and no second starts beside it. Nothing else changes while it starts: it is handed none of the game, and the windows show the game’s own text. Once it has connected, the relay says so (Wire::begin), and at the actor’s next turn, half a second at most, it is handed its login and the game, and the windows turn to its text (Event::LichBegun).
