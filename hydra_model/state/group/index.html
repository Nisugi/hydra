Who is grouped with you.

§Patterns match the prose, not the markup

Every group line names its participants as links:

<a exist="-123" noun="Dicate">Dicate</a> joins your group.

ChunkLine::links yields LinkKind::Exist with id and noun already typed, so a pattern here only has to say which event a line is; the participants are the links, in order. No pattern re-tokenizes the markup to recover exist= and noun=.

§The members are ids, not names

A group holds exist ids. Two characters can display the same name – the possessive pronoun in adds you to <a>his</a> group is itself a link, with the leader’s id – so the id is the identity and the name is decoration.

§Who leads is three answers, not two

Nobody may have said yet, you may lead, or someone else may. Spelling “unknown” and “you” alike would leave “am I the leader?” with no answer, and every group role is read off it. Leader keeps the three apart.

“You” is prose, not a link, in every group line (designates you, You are leading), so the classifier needs no id to find it. The character’s own id (Character::exist_id) is for a line that names you by a link anyway: Group::apply reads that link as you, never as a member.
